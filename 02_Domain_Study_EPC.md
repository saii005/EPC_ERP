# 02. Domain Study: EPC (Engineering, Procurement, and Construction)

## 1. Introduction to EPC Business Model
Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) is a prominent form of contracting agreement in the construction and heavy engineering industries. Under an EPC contract, a contractor is entrusted with executing a project under a single responsibility agreement—often structured as a **Turnkey Contract**.

Key characteristics of EPC projects include:
* **Lump Sum Turnkey (LSTK):** High financial risk transferred to the contractor in exchange for fixed-price certainty for the owner.
* **Complex Multi-Disciplinary Work:** Involves tight coordination between engineering design, global sourcing/logistics, heavy civil/mechanical construction, and stringent commissioning.
* **Long Project Lifecycles:** Spanning anywhere from 18 months to 5+ years per capital project.

---

## 2. The Three Pillars of EPC

### A. Engineering (The Intellectual Foundation)
Engineering sets the specifications, bills of materials (BOM), and execution blueprints. It is divided into:
* **FEED (Front-End Engineering Design):** Preliminary engineering establishing technical requirements and rough cost estimates.
* **Detailed Engineering:** Producing Issued-For-Construction (IFC) drawings, P&IDs, 3D piping models, structural calculations, and equipment specifications.

### B. Procurement (The Supply Chain & Logistics Engine)
Procurement ensures the right materials arrive at the right time and budget:
* Global sourcing and vendor qualification.
* Technical and commercial bid evaluations (TBE/CBE).
* Long-lead item management, inspection at vendor shops (FAT - Factory Acceptance Testing), customs clearance, and site logistics.

### C. Construction & Commissioning (Execution & Handover)
Site execution transforms paper designs into physical assets:
* Civil works, structural steel erection, heavy equipment setting, piping, electrical, and instrumentation.
* Pre-commissioning, commissioning, Performance Guarantee Tests (PGT), and handover to the client/operations team.

---

## 3. Key Business Challenges in EPC
* **Cost Overruns & Margin Erosion:** Unforeseen site conditions, material price volatility, and scope creep.
* **Schedule Delays:** Bottlenecks in engineering approvals or delayed long-lead equipment cascading into site downtime.
* **HSE & Quality Risks:** Zero-accident mandates and strict adherence to ASME, API, ISO, and statutory standards.
